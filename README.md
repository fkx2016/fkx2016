# Frank Kurka

I build AI-native software for human-AI partnership: operator tools, local-first systems, web automation, and new publishing formats.

After more than 45 years in computing, I am interested in what comes after the chatbot: software in which people supply purpose, judgment, and direction while AI handles more of the operational complexity.

## Current work

### PubDesk + PubStream

A local-first publication control room and supervised distribution engine. PubDesk brings drafts, editorial decisions, account readiness, platform variants, and publication evidence into one workspace. PubStream prepares and distributes approved work across web, newsletter, and social destinations while preserving human control over consequential actions.

**Status:** Active private pilot

### [Fieldwork](https://fieldwork.chat)

A shared workspace for working with an AI operator on a real project—not merely chatting with a model. Fieldwork keeps the question, project context, working conversation, decisions, and delivery together from inquiry through completion.

**Status:** Live

### [Kurka Labs](https://kurkalabs.dev)

The public home for my independent AI research, products, experiments, and writing. Kurka Labs explores local-first AI, human-AI partnership, agent-operated software, and tools that make complex systems usable without forcing people to master every underlying console.

**Status:** Live and building in public

### Automated Book Publishing

A working BookX pipeline that turns one structured manuscript into PDF, EPUB, DOCX, and HTML, then generates proofs and checks the resulting files. It automates repeatable production work while keeping editorial review, cover art, commercial decisions, and storefront submission under human supervision.

**Status:** Working pipeline in active development

## Principles

- AI should expand human agency, not quietly replace it.
- Local and private operation should remain practical options.
- Automation should be observable, reversible, and honest about its limits.
- People should express intent; software should absorb more of the platform complexity.
- A working artifact is more valuable than a speculative claim.
